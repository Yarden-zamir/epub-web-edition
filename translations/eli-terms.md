# Eli Raz: Hebrew terms and style

Source for the Hebrew translation of "The Life Story of the Dead Sea" (unit a06).
Every form below comes from Eli's own Hebrew chapters. The element ID shows where.
Use these forms. If a term is not here, use the form that the rest of the book uses.

## Sentence style

- Long, formal sentences with several clauses. Commas before relative clauses ("…, שהיה…").
- Literary and biblical register: "לא כל שכן", "אך טבעי הוא", "יצאו לו מוניטין", "הפכוהו", "איפוא/אפוא".
- Dry irony and an exclamation mark at the end of a point (a07-p008 "וזאת באמצע המאה ה- 19!", a36-p039, a36-p073).
- Personal voice in the first person singular when he talks about his own work (a14-p015, a36-p009). First person plural for "we humans" (a36-p052).
- Quotes in double quotes "…"; names of documents, plans and books in single quotes '…' ('תעלת הימים', 'ספר תבואות הארץ', 'המוביל ההפוך').
- Glosses in brackets after a technical or foreign term: "אגם טרמינלי" after "חסר ניקוז חיצוני, היינו" (a35-p003); "'היפוך' (overturn)" (a07-p025).
- Latin species names stay in Latin script (Haloarcula marismortui, a35-p032).

## Citations in footnotes

- Translate the prose of a footnote. Keep the citation of an English publication exactly as printed, in Latin script.
- Footnotes that are only an English citation stay untranslated and are not in the translation file (a06-fn5, fn9, fn10, fn11, fn15, fn19–fn23, fn25).
- For a Hebrew publication, use the Hebrew title only when the book prints it: Elazari-Volcani thesis "מחקר על החיים בים המלח" (sources-p002), Raz 1993 'ספר ים המלח', רשות שמורות הטבע (sources-p040), the HaMaarag report (a35-src1).
- Otherwise keep the printed English title and write "(בעברית)" for "(Hebrew)". Do not back-translate a title. The original Hebrew title is not known.
